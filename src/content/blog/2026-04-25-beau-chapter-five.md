That night, Beau dreamed.

He dreamed the way dogs dream… his legs twitching, his breath quickening, small sounds escaping from his throat that were not quite barks and not quite words. Clementine, half-awake in her bed above him, reached down and rested her hand on his side, and his breathing slowed, but the dream did not stop.

It went deeper.

------

*The old-place.*

*No house-shapes. No road-lines. No mouth-sounds anywhere. The island is just itself… rock and salt-water and wind that has never been interrupted by anything taller than a standing stone.*

*I am not this shape. I am a different shape. Longer legs. Thicker fur. A jaw that could break the bones of the red deer that move across the high moor in their slow, enormous herds. My ears both stand. My eyes are not brown. They are the colour of the winter sky before the snow comes… grey, and old, and full of seeing.*

*But I am still me. The duty is still the duty. The cold-smell is still the cold-smell. Only there is more of it. So much more. It moves across the land like weather. It pools in the low-places where the rivers slow and the ground goes soft. It gathers at the edges of the standing stones where the hill-people will one day make their circles, though the hill-people are not here yet. Nothing is here yet that walks on two legs and makes mouth-sounds and builds walls against the dark.*

*There are others like me. Not many. We do not run together. We are not pack-things. We are each of us posted to a place… a ridge, a ford, a cave-mouth, a stretch of shore where the sea meets the land and the meeting is not always peaceful. We know each other by scent and by the old knowing. We do not have names. Names come later, with the mouth-sound-makers. We have positions. We have posts.*

*My post is a hill. Not a tall hill. A low, wide hill with a flat stone at its top where the wind has worn a shallow bowl into the rock that fills with rain and holds the sky like a mirror. From this hill I can see the sea in three directions. I can see the places where the land dips and the between-places open like mouths in the earth.*

*The cold-smell comes from the north. It always comes from the north. Not because the north is where it lives but because the north is where the walls of the world are thinnest, where the dark presses closest, where the space between what is and what wants to be is narrow enough to slip through.*

*I stand on my hill and I watch it come.*

*It is bigger here. In the bright-child’s time, the cold-smell is small. Squeezed into floor-cracks and coat-corners. Diminished by the house-shapes and the warm-fires and the centuries of mouth-sound-makers who have built and named and known and pushed the between-places to the edges. But here, in the before-time, the cold-smell is the size of the sky. It moves across the moor like a tide. It has no shape because it does not need one. It is the wanting itself… formless, patient, endless.*

*What does it want?*

*The same thing it always wants. The same thing it will want ten thousand winters from now when it presses its face against the floor-crack in a house in a village that does not exist yet. It wants the fire. It wants the brightness. It wants the living warmth of the world and it wants to drink it the way the sea drinks rivers… slowly, completely, until there is nothing left but salt and silence.*

*There is no bright-child here. There are no children at all. But there is fire. The world itself is fire… the green fire of growing things, the red fire of the deer’s blood, the white fire of the standing stones that hum with a sound too low for any ear but mine. Life is fire. The cold-smell wants the fire. All fire. Any fire.*

*I stand on my hill. The cold-smell reaches the base of the hill and stops. It does not stop because it is afraid of me. It stops because I am here, and being here is enough. Presence is the ward. The oldest ward. Before the loud-laughing, before the barking-wall, before any wall at all, there was simply this: a guardian, standing, refusing to move.*

*The cold-smell pushes. I hold. It pushes harder. I hold. This is the whole of the duty. This is what it has always been. Not fighting. Not winning. Holding. Standing at the mouth of things and not stepping aside.*

*The wind comes off the sea and it carries salt and the cry of the white birds and the smell of distances I will never cross. The sky turns. The stars come through… the same stars, the same old-lights that will shine over the bright-child’s village in a time so far from now that even I cannot feel the shape of it.*

*I do not know about the bright-child yet. I do not know about houses or kitchens or the foot-thing-with-the-hole or the sound of a girl laughing so hard her fire blazes. I do not know about the floppy ear. I do not know about toast.*

*I know the hill. I know the cold. I know the holding.*

*It is enough.*

*The cold-smell thins. It pulls back toward the north, toward the thin-places where the world frays. It will come again. It always comes again. But tonight it has met a guardian on a hill and the guardian did not move and so it goes to try another place, another edge, another mouth.*

*I lie down on the flat stone. The rain-bowl is full of stars. I rest my chin on my paws… different paws, larger paws, paws that have never known a kitchen floor… and I close my eyes that are not brown yet.*

*The hill holds. The stone holds. The duty holds.*

*Different legs. Different fur. Same love.*

*Wait.*

*Not same love. Not yet. The love comes later. The love comes with the mouth-sound-makers and their small warm hands and their habit of giving you three names and a piece of toast when they are sorry. The love comes with the bright-child and the floppy ear and the sound of the True-name in the old-mother’s mouth.*

*Here, in the before-time, there is no love. There is only the duty. And the duty is enough because it has to be.*

*But.*

*But I think I am waiting for something. I think the duty knows that one day it will not be alone. That one day a girl will sit on a kitchen floor in her sleeping-clothes and put her hand on the old ear and say I know you weren’t being bad and the duty will crack open and inside it will be something so bright that even the cold-smell will flinch.*

*I think the duty is waiting for the love the way the rain-bowl on the stone is waiting for the stars.*

*I do not have words for this. I will not have words for this for a very long time.*

*But the knowing is there. Whole. Not built from parts.*

*Waiting.*

------

Beau’s legs twitched one last time. A small sound came from his throat… not a bark, not a whimper, but something older, something that belonged to a hill on an island before the island had a name.

Clementine’s hand rested on his side. His breathing steadied.

“Shh,” she said, half in her own sleep. “It’s all right. You’re home.”

He was.

For now, he was.
