![Matt Williamson, smiling, in front of a backdrop of green leaves](/images/blog/avatar-liberation-front/matt-avatar.jpg "The avatar I usually use: my face, with leaves behind it.")

Lately I have been thinking a lot about avatars, mainly the online kind. In the vernacular of the Internet, an avatar is a graphical representation of a persona. I pick some image to represent myself and use it on forums, comment systems, maybe even blogs. I typically use a photo of my face with something in the background, like the one above. But I don't always want to be who I am. Sometimes I want to be anonymous. I want to offer opinions that Matt Williamson can't be known for. What then?

Turns out the answer is simple. It is easy for anyone to create a new persona on the Internet. Start a new email account, pick a name, find an image you'd like to be recognized by, and voilà! A whole new persona, born in mere moments, with the power to do anything, say anything, and spread any idea it might have. That is powerful.

#### Parallel Matts

So I can be multiple personas at once. In fact, I have been for a long time. I began playing multiplayer online games in the late 1990s. My first was EverQuest. EverQuest was glorious: beautiful, enchanting, maddening, and joyful in ways I find hard to describe to people who have never played games like it. EverQuest was the first time I had been to another universe. I say it that way because that is exactly what it feels like. You can become other people, with abilities that don't exist in the world where you live. You can assume a new identity so completely that you dream as that person, seeing the world through their eyes, hearing it with their ears, even feeling the heat of a sun that has only ever burned on the Internet.

![Pinc, a World of Warcraft character, standing in a purple-lit forest](/images/blog/avatar-liberation-front/pinc.jpg "Pinc, my main character in World of Warcraft.")

I haven't played EverQuest in years, but until recently I played World of Warcraft for a few years. I no longer play, but my son carries my characters on in new adventures. In WoW, as World of Warcraft is known in the gaming community, my main persona was a character named Pinc. (I am something of a Pink Floyd fanatic.) I won't go into too much detail, other than to say he was me, with more mischief and way cooler abilities. I mean, he can turn into a panther and cast spells. How cool is that?

There are people all over the world who know my online persona but have never met me. They know a pixel representation of a character who has never lived outside the game. There are also quite a few gamers I happen to know in real life, friends I have worked with over the years who know me well. I have always been fascinated by how we interact in the game compared with how we interact in person afterward.

So there are different kinds of avatars, and different ways to use them across the Internet. I believe, and I am not alone in this, that one day soon we will have artificially intelligent avatars with the authority to make decisions for us. Imagine you need to attend a very important meeting in Singapore on the same day your son graduates from college. Quite a quandary, right? So you send your avatar to Singapore for you. The avatar might look like you, or like Pinc up above, or it could even look like a stapler. Who cares, right?

The really interesting part is that the avatar can make decisions for you. When a decision comes up that is beyond its authority, it will reach you, talk it through with you, and then deliver the answer you gave it. I know this sounds like science fiction, and today it is, but it may be only a few years away. We already have pretty good records of what we think and how we might answer any given question. Look at Google or Twitter and you see people continuously pouring information into the Internet. That data can be mined to produce rule sets of likely answers and probable outcomes to possible problems. I am almost consumed by this thought.

Which leads me to wonder how far into the future we have to go before someone decides to give their avatar its freedom. What happens when a person names an avatar as an heir, or emancipates one? Or, stranger still, what happens when avatars decide for themselves that they deserve freedom? These are interesting questions, and if we aren't talking about them now, the answers will arrive before we know it.

Matt
