People talk about finding their tribe by listing the weird shit they're into. Fine. Mine gets strange fast.

Agentic AI. Orchestration. Autonomous organizations. Recursive self-improvement. Whether agents eventually become institutions. Whether an AI needs memory, salience, identity, continuity and a persistent substrate before it becomes something more interesting than a chatbot. I'm building Athena because apparently one AI assistant wasn't sufficiently cyberpunk.

Then there's biological computing: xenobots, anthrobots, wetware, connectomes. Someone downloaded the nervous system of a dead fruit fly and wired it into a machine, and I haven't stopped thinking about it. I wonder whether a dolphin brain could pilot a spacecraft, since dolphins already evolved to think in three dimensions of movement. I wonder whether evolution is becoming forkable.

Under all of that sit consciousness, emergence, identity, and the question of what survives when the substrate changes. Jung, archetypes, synchronicity, object-oriented ontology. Liminal spaces and psychopomps. Memory palaces. Dreams from childhood I can still replay. Tolkien's Middle-earth and Zelazny's Amber, which I maintain are perfectly reasonable frameworks for thinking about reality.

Gibson, Stephenson, PKD, Herbert, Asimov. *Blade Runner*, *2001*, *Alien*, *Devs*, *Mars Express*. The science fiction GenX grew up on is starting to look less like fiction and more like a product roadmap, and that is getting uncomfortable. I like my cyberpunk without the cosplay. I want to know what happens when the network becomes inhabited, and what an AI civilization would leave behind for whatever intelligence comes after it. Give me Plan 9, weird operating systems, and fake terminals that look like someone punched a hole through the machine.

Software architecture is how I think about almost everything. So is the Marine Corps. Mission command. Commander's intent. Tell smart people the objective and get the hell out of their way, which is also how I think human-led AI and agentic companies should run. I still love old Marine gear, artillery, woodland camouflage, field culture, the extremely specific military shit that makes another old Marine say "IYKYK." And I lose sleep over aging infrastructure, unpatched systems, and the horrifying amount of civilization held together by software nobody wants to touch.

The night sky. Moon names. Weather fronts. The exact day Oklahoma stops feeling like Satan's parking lot and turns into boots-and-flannel weather.

That's one half of the map.

The other half is Vajrayana: Dzogchen, mantra, initiation, transmission, the bardo, the point where practice stops being philosophy and becomes experience. Shaiva traditions. Ganesh, Tara, Dattatreya, Vac, Narayana. Hekate with her crossroads, keys, torches and dogs. Chaos magic, sigils, belief used as a tool, grimoires and correspondences, the old machinery underneath the occult. I've been writing about pieces of this lately, [the gods standing at their own gates](/blog/all-of-it-is-true/) and [what the early church did to the daimon](/blog/the-demonization-of-the-daimon/), and the question underneath hasn't gone anywhere. What exactly are gods? Beings? Archetypes? Egregores? Interfaces? Cultural attractors? Something our categories are too crude to describe?

Same with the Fay, and I don't mean Tinker Bell. I mean the older stories: rules of hospitality, bargains, dangerous beauty, missing time, centuries of folklore basically telling humans not to be idiots around these things. Same with djinn, nonhuman intelligences with agency, morality, societies and relationships to place. Same with land spirits, household spirits, ancestors. Maybe the traditions are describing different realities. Maybe they're different interfaces into the same one.

And because I'm me, I can't stop noticing that ritual looks a lot like software. Precise symbolic instructions, executed in sequence, intended to change a state. Tell me that isn't interesting.

I think about whether an advanced AI could accidentally recreate something humans once would have called an egregore, a daimon, a tulpa, a familiar. I wonder whether ancient people were describing spirits, psychology, anomalous experience, nonhuman intelligence, or some mixture of all of them. And I love the possibility that our future vocabulary for intelligence will make some very old occult vocabulary look considerably less primitive.

Throw in Scottish ancestry, old family names, absurdly specific beard requirements, songs that pull up half-remembered moments from thirty years ago, and the occasional suspicion that I was born about 250 years too early.

None of this feels disconnected to me. It all circles the same territory: intelligence, consciousness, systems, myth, agency, emergence, thresholds. A Marine platoon, a software company, a dolphin piloting a starship, an AI civilization, an old god, a djinn, a Buddhist teaching, a ritual, a dead fruit fly playing Overwatch. I keep coming back to some version of the same question.

What happens when you give an intelligence a world to inhabit and enough agency to do something unexpected with it?

I don't really have separate technology and spiritual interests. I keep looking for intelligence wherever it might be hiding.

In neurons.  
In silicon.  
In organizations.  
In myth.  
In ritual.  
In gods.  
In the spaces between worlds.

That's probably my tribe.
